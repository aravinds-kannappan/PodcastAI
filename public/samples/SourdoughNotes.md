# Sourdough field notes, week 6

I keep these notes on the counter, not in a lab notebook. The starter is six weeks old, fed with rye at night and bread flour in the morning.

## What changed this week

The culture smells like green apple and yogurt instead of nail polish. That is the finding I care about. When the night temperature sat at 68 F, a 1:2:2 feed peaked in about five hours. At 62 F the same feed needed closer to nine.

Bulk fermentation of a 72 percent hydration dough finished when the dough jiggled like a waterbed and had risen about 40 percent, not doubled. I keep overproofing when I wait for a doubling because home bakers copy recipes written for warmer kitchens.

## Numbers I actually wrote down

- Inoculation: 20 percent starter by flour weight.
- Salt: 2.1 percent.
- Loaf A (Dutch oven, 475 F, 20 minutes lid on): ear formed, crust mahogany, crumb 4 on a 5-point openness scale.
- Loaf B (uncovered sheet, same dough): paler crust, tighter crumb, still good sandwich bread.

I found that a 30-minute autolyse before adding salt made the dough less sticky than mixing everything at once. That is a small result and I would not publish it, but it changed how I bake on weeknights.

## Limitations

This is n = 4 loaves, one kitchen, one bag of flour. Humidity in this apartment swings with the laundry. Anyone copying these percentages in Phoenix in July should expect a faster peak and a sourer loaf.

## Takeaway I would tell a friend

Feed by smell and volume, not by the clock on the recipe blog. If it smells like solvent, you waited too long. If it smells like apples, mix the dough.
